## Starting point

New members of the export trucking team learned the job only through hands-on training. There was **no standard reference** to go back to. What they learned depended on who trained them and which problems happened to come up that month.

## Root cause

The knowledge was there, but it lived in people's heads and in scattered documents. Every function involved in export trucking (shipment control, the trucking planner, EMKL transporters, the warehouse) knew its own part, and nobody had written down how the parts connect.

## Solution

A manual book written for one reader: a newcomer who needs to become productive quickly. It covers eight areas:

1. Overview
2. Process flow
3. Documents
4. Links to other functions
5. Roles
6. Systems
7. Common issues
8. Work instructions, step by step, including the system screens used

How it was delivered:

1. **Define the content list** with the manager.
2. **Collect materials** that already existed.
3. **Interview each PIC** to capture what was never written down.
4. **Compile each chapter.**
5. **Review and gather feedback** from the team.
6. **Revise and finalize.**

## Before and after

- **Before:** learning depended entirely on on-the-job training and on who was available to explain.
- **After:** every newcomer starts with the same reference, from the big-picture workflow down to the exact steps in the system.

## What this shows

Standardization is not only about SOPs for experienced staff. Writing the process down for a newcomer forces every handover between functions to be made explicit, and that is often where improvement opportunities show up.
