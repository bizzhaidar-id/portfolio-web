## Starting point

Export inland trucking moved containers from the mill to the port through several transporter vendors. The vendors with the lowest rate, the **Top 1 tier**, carried only **25%** of the volume in 2023. Every container that went to a higher-rate vendor instead added cost without adding service.

The target was SMART:

- **Specific:** raise the use of the lowest-rate (Top 1) vendors
- **Measurable:** Top 1 share from 25% to 53%
- **Achievable:** within the budget allocation set at HQ
- **Reasonable:** directly supports the logistics cost KPI
- **Time-bound:** six months, January to June 2024

## Root cause

A 4M analysis showed the problem was not the vendors' willingness. It was how allocation, cargo readiness and stuffing interacted:

1. **Method: only two vendors in the Top 1 tier.** Allocation depended on agreements with just two companies, so there was no room to grow their share.
2. **Material: Top 1 trucks were limited and not always ready.** Their policy is that trucks cannot wait for late cargo, so any delay pushed the job to a more expensive vendor.
3. **Method: uneven stuffing workload.** Orders were confirmed suddenly, the warehouse filled up and stuffing took longer than planned.
4. **Method: the planned ready date kept changing.** Unclear payment and cargo reallocated to higher-priority orders made the plan unreliable.

## Solution

Six countermeasures, each mapped to the root cause it fixes:

| # | Countermeasure | Fixes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Add Top 1 vendors.** Negotiated extra trucks from existing Top 1 vendors and widened the pool from 2 to 5. | Cause 1, 2 |
| 2 | **Daily utilization check.** Compare each vendor's actual use against its allocation every day. | Cause 1 |
| 3 | **Forecast early, book early.** Customer service shares forecasts early, and dummy bookings secure slots before orders arrive. | Cause 4 |
| 4 | **Payment cut-off.** Customers pay by the 10th so shipments can be arranged by the 15th. | Cause 4 |
| 5 | **Prioritize ready orders.** Orders with a confirmed schedule go first, and a daily review pulls forward faster sailings. | Cause 3, 4 |
| 6 | **Rebalance stuffing capacity.** Shift manpower follows container arrivals, and a gate control tower speeds up turnaround. | Cause 3 |

Three of the 22 logistics automation projects running at the time supported this directly. A container arrival auto-warning and real-time container tracking gave the team early visibility, which made stuffing smoother.

## Results

Measured January to October 2024 against the 53% target:

- **Top 1 vendor usage averaged 53.5%**, up from 25%, beating the target.
- **Inland trucking cost per MT fell 21%** compared with 2023.
- The project won a **gold medal** in the company SGA competition.

## Standardize and roll out

The countermeasures became work instructions so the result does not depend on the project team: the daily vendor utilization check, expediting to the fastest schedule, dummy booking ahead of orders, the gate queue control tower and stuffing manpower planning.

The approach was then replicated to **domestic trucking and a sister mill**. Next on the list: online booking confirmation and a vendor allocation monitoring system.

## What this shows

The obvious fix was "tell the team to use cheaper vendors". It would not have worked, because the cheaper vendors could not wait for late cargo. The gains came from fixing cargo readiness and planning upstream, so the cheaper option became the reliable one.
